Question #1

Light Control:
Where They Differ Most

When customers are deciding between the two, light control is usually the first thing they ask about. Here’s how each one handles it.

Zebra blinds

☀️

Adjust the light without raising the shade

Line up the solid bands for privacy and soft light, offset them so the sheer bands let you see out, or roll the whole shade up. That flexibility is the main reason homeowners pick zebra.

👁

Filtered light and privacy at the same time

Partly offsetting the bands lets daylight in while still breaking up the view into the room — handy for street-facing rooms.

🌙

Light-filtering or blackout zebra

Choose light-filtering zebra for everyday rooms, or blackout zebra fabric for bedrooms. See our blackout blinds.

Roller shades

↕️

Open, closed or partly lowered

A roller shade covers whatever part of the window it’s lowered over. The covered part gets the fabric’s light level; the rest is open glass.

🎨

The fabric sets the light level

Light-filtering fabric softens daylight, solar fabric cuts glare while keeping your view, and blackout fabric darkens the room.

✅

Simple and predictable

Each shade has one fabric, so you choose the light level you want most for that room and it stays that way.

Our take: if you want to change the light through the day without raising the shade, zebra blinds give you more control — that’s why most homeowners we work with choose them. If you already know how much light you want in a room, a roller shade in the right fabric does that job with a simpler look.

Question #2

Heat Control:
Summer Sun & Winter Windows

The second thing customers ask. The honest answer: the fabric you choose matters more than whether it’s a zebra blind or a roller shade.

Zebra blinds

🌞

Align the bands on sunny afternoons

With the solid bands lined up, more of the window is covered by solid fabric, which helps cut direct sun and glare in south- and west-facing rooms.

🧶

Two layers of fabric when closed

A closed zebra blind puts two layers of fabric between the room and the glass.

⚠️

Sheer bands let sun through

When the bands are offset for the view, sunlight comes in through the sheer stripes.

Roller shades

🌅

Solar fabric for sun and glare

Solar roller shades are made to cut glare and UV while you keep your view — a common choice for large, sunny windows.

🌙

Blackout fabric blocks the most

A blackout roller shade blocks the most light of any roller fabric, which also helps keep strong sun out.

⬜

Full coverage when lowered

One continuous sheet of fabric covers the glass, with no sheer stripes.

Our take: for summer sun, a solar or blackout roller shade or a zebra blind with the bands aligned will all help, and we’ll help you compare fabrics at your consultation. A good fit at the edges matters too. If keeping a room warm in winter is your top priority, ask us about honeycomb blinds as well, since they’re designed for insulation.

Question #3

Durability:
Which Holds Up Better?

The third question we hear. Both styles use the same basic mechanism — fabric on a roller — so both are durable with normal, everyday use. The differences are in the details.

Zebra blinds

📏

Two layers that need to stay aligned

The sheer and solid bands have to line up evenly across the window. That depends on the shade being made square and installed level, which we check on every install.

🧹

A little more care when cleaning

Because there are two fabric layers, gentle dusting keeps them looking their best. Avoid soaking the fabric, since the bands are held under tension on the roller.

Roller shades

⚙️

The simplest design

One layer of fabric on one roller means fewer parts to keep aligned.

🧹

Easy to look after

A flat fabric surface is easy to dust and wipe, which helps in busy rooms.

Our take: roller shades have the edge on simplicity. But for either style, how well it holds up comes down to the quality of the fabric and hardware and how carefully it’s measured and installed — not the style itself. Cordless and motorized operation also means no cords to wear or tangle. Every shade we make is custom-made to your measurements and installed by our own team — see how our installation works.

Privacy

Privacy,
Day and Night

A common follow-up question: are zebra blinds see-through at night? Only the sheer bands — here’s how privacy works with each style.

Zebra blinds

☀️

Daytime

With the bands offset, the sheer stripes let you see out while still softening the view in. Line up the solid bands for more privacy.

🌙

At night

With your lights on, people outside can see in through the sheer bands. Align the solid bands in the evening; with blackout zebra fabric, that blocks the view almost completely.

Roller shades

🎨

Light-filtering and solar fabrics

Great for daytime privacy and keeping a view, but they can become see-through at night when your lights are on.

🔒

Blackout fabric

Blocks the view in both directions, day or night — a popular choice for bedrooms and street-facing windows.

Our take: for rooms you use at night, either style works as long as you pick the right fabric. Zebra gives you both modes in one shade; with roller shades, choose blackout where night-time privacy matters.

Care

Cleaning
and Maintenance

Neither style needs much upkeep, but roller shades are a little easier to keep clean.

Zebra blinds

🧹

Regular dusting

Use a soft brush or a vacuum brush attachment, since dust can settle between the two fabric layers.

💧

Spot-clean marks

A damp cloth with mild soap works on most fabrics. Don’t soak the fabric.

Roller shades

🧹

Regular dusting

One flat surface, so a soft cloth or vacuum brush attachment is all it needs.

💧

Wipe marks

Wipe marks with a damp cloth and check the fabric’s care advice first, since some fabrics only need spot cleaning.

Our take: if easy cleaning is high on your list — a kitchen, for example — a roller shade is the easier option. Our blind cleaning and care guide has more tips for both.

Cost

Cost:
Is There a Big Difference?

No. The price difference between zebra blinds and roller shades is very small. Because every shade is made to measure, we can only give an accurate quote once we have your window sizes — which is why we measure for free.

What changes the price

📐

Number and size of windows

Larger windows and more of them mean more material and more installation time — for either style.

🎨

Fabric

Light-filtering, solar and blackout fabrics are priced differently within each style.

 

⚡

Manual, cordless or motorized

Motorized shades cost more than manual ones, and this applies to both zebra and roller.

🚪

Specialty openings

Wide patio doors and very tall windows take more planning and hardware than a standard window.

Pros & Cons

Zebra & Roller Blinds
Pros and Cons

Everything above, boiled down.

Zebra blinds pros and cons

Pros

  • Most flexible light control — adjust it without raising the shade
  • Filtered daylight and privacy at the same time
  • Modern look and one of our most requested styles
  • Light-filtering and blackout fabrics, cordless and motorized options

Cons

  • The sheer bands can be seen through at night when offset
  • Two fabric layers take a little more care to clean
  • More to keep aligned than a single roller shade
  • The striped look isn’t to everyone’s taste

Roller blinds pros and cons

Pros

  • Simplest design, with fewer parts to keep aligned
  • Clean, minimal look — the usual choice for businesses
  • Light-filtering, solar and blackout fabrics
  • Easiest to clean; cordless and motorized options

Cons

  • One light level per shade — to change it, you raise or lower the shade
  • Letting light in means giving up some privacy, unless you choose solar or light-filtering fabric
  • Light-filtering and solar fabrics can be seen through at night

Common Questions

Zebra vs Roller FAQ

Are zebra blinds better than roller blinds?

Neither is better for every window. Zebra blinds give you more flexible light control, because you can shift the sheer and solid bands without raising the shade — that’s why most homeowners we work with choose them. Roller shades are simpler, with a single clean sheet of fabric, which is why most businesses choose them. The price difference is very small, so the deciding factor is usually how you want to use the room.

Are zebra blinds worth it?

For most homes, yes — if you like to adjust light and privacy through the day. Because the price difference between zebra blinds and roller shades is very small, you aren’t paying much for that extra flexibility. If you only ever want a room fully open or fully covered, a roller shade does that job just as well with a simpler look.

Do zebra blinds block out light? Can they be blackout?

Light-filtering zebra blinds soften and reduce light rather than blocking it fully. If you want a dark room, yes — we offer blackout zebra fabric: with the bands aligned, it blocks light and outside view almost completely, while still letting you open the bands for daytime light. See our blackout blinds for more options.

Are zebra blinds see-through at night?

Only the sheer bands. When the bands are offset for a view and your lights are on, people outside can see in through the sheer stripes. Line up the solid bands in the evening and you get privacy; for the most privacy at night, choose blackout zebra fabric.

Which is better for heat control, zebra or roller?

The fabric matters more than the style. A solar or blackout roller shade, or a zebra blind with the solid bands aligned, helps cut direct sun and glare in summer. A closed zebra blind also puts two layers of fabric over the glass. If keeping a room warm in winter is your main goal, ask us about honeycomb blinds as well, since they’re designed for insulation.

Which lasts longer, zebra blinds or roller shades?

Both use the same basic mechanism — fabric on a roller — so both are durable with normal use. Roller shades are the simpler of the two, with one layer of fabric and fewer parts to keep aligned. Zebra blinds have two fabric layers that need to stay lined up, which comes down to the shade being made square and installed level. For either style, fabric and hardware quality and a careful install matter more than the style itself.

Which is more expensive, zebra blinds or roller shades?

There’s very little price difference between the two. Because every shade is made to measure, an accurate quote depends on your window sizes, plus the fabric and whether you want cordless or motorized operation. We measure for free and give you a fixed, no-obligation quote — and we can quote both styles so you can compare.

Are zebra blinds still in style?

Yes. Zebra blinds are one of our most requested styles, and their clean, modern look suits Metro Vancouver condos and newer homes. If you prefer something plainer, a roller shade gives an even more minimal look.

Which is easier to clean?

Roller shades. They’re one flat surface that you can dust or wipe. Zebra blinds have two fabric layers, so dust can settle between them; a soft brush or a vacuum brush attachment works well, and marks can be spot-cleaned with a damp cloth and mild soap. Our blind cleaning guide has more tips.

Can zebra blinds and roller shades be motorized or cordless?

Yes, both. We offer cordless options for zebra blinds and roller shades, and both can be motorized — operated by remote, wall switch or app, with smart-home integration available.
