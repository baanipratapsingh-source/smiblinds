Cordless Blinds

What Are
Cordless Blinds?

Cordless blinds have no lift cord or loop. You raise and lower them by hand, and a spring-tension mechanism in the headrail holds them wherever you stop. They're the simplest, most popular choice for homes.

👆

How They Work

Push the bottom rail up to raise the blind, pull it down to lower it. On zebra blinds you lower or raise to line up the sheer and solid bands; on vertical blinds, a wand turns and draws the vanes.

👶

Child- & Pet-Safe

No reachable cords means no cord loops for small children or pets to get caught in — the reason cordless is the standard for nurseries and kids' rooms.

✨

Clean, Modern Look

Nothing hanging down the side of the window. Cordless cellular blinds, faux wood blinds and blackout blinds look tidy from inside and out.

🏠

Best For

Bedrooms, nurseries, kitchens and any window you can easily reach. Very wide or tall windows are usually easier with motorized.

Power Options

Motorized Blinds

What Are
Motorized Blinds?

A small motor in the headrail raises, lowers or rotates the blind when you press a remote, a wall switch or a button in an app — or ask Alexa, Google Home or Apple HomeKit. They're cordless too, so they're just as child-safe. Full details on our motorized blinds page.

🔋

How They're Powered

Choose hardwired, rechargeable battery, replaceable battery or solar. Battery and solar options don't need to be plugged in, so they work on windows with no outlet nearby.

📱

Remote, App & Voice

Control one blind or a whole room at once, and set schedules — close west-facing shades before the afternoon sun, open them at sunrise.

🪓

Easiest to Use

No lifting, no reaching. A good choice for anyone with limited mobility or strength, and for heavy blinds on large windows.

🏢

Best For

High and hard-to-reach windows, large windows, sliding glass doors, rooms with many windows, and smart homes.

Corded Blinds

Corded Blinds:
For Commercial Use Only

🏢 Offices · restaurants · retail · clinics · rental & strata common areas

Corded blinds are raised and lowered with a lift cord or a continuous cord or chain loop at the side of the window. They're simple, durable and cost-effective across a large number of windows — which is why they're still common in offices, restaurants and other businesses.

For homes, we make cordless or motorized blinds only. Since May 2022, Health Canada's Corded Window Coverings Regulations have strictly limited reachable cords and loops on window coverings for household use, to protect young children from strangulation. Corded products sold and used only by businesses fall outside those rules, so we offer corded controls for commercial projects only.

Furnishing a business? See our commercial blinds page. Furnishing a home? Go with cordless or motorized.

Common Questions

Blind Controls FAQ

What is the difference between corded, cordless and motorized blinds?

Corded blinds are raised and lowered with a cord or chain loop. Cordless blinds have no cord: you lift or lower the bottom rail by hand and a spring-tension mechanism holds them in place. Motorized blinds use a small motor you control with a remote, wall switch, app or voice assistant. For homes we offer cordless and motorized; corded blinds are for commercial use only.

Can every blind style be made cordless or motorized?

Yes. All of our styles — zebra blinds, roller shades, blackout blinds, honeycomb (cellular) blinds, faux wood blinds and vertical blinds — can be made with cordless or motorized controls, and with corded controls for commercial projects.

Why are corded blinds only available for commercial use?

Since May 2022, Health Canada's Corded Window Coverings Regulations have strictly limited reachable cords and cord loops on window coverings for household use, to reduce the strangulation risk to young children. Corded window coverings sold to and used only by businesses fall outside those rules, so we make corded blinds for commercial spaces only. For homes, choose cordless or motorized.

Are corded blinds illegal in Canada?

Not outright, but window coverings sold for home use must meet strict limits on cord length and loops, so traditional corded blinds are no longer a home product. That's why we only offer them for offices, restaurants, retail and other commercial spaces.

Are cordless blinds safer for kids and pets?

Yes. With no reachable cords or loops, cordless blinds remove the cord strangulation hazard. Motorized blinds are cordless too, so they're equally child- and pet-safe.

How do cordless blinds work?

To raise a cordless blind, push the bottom rail up; to lower it, pull it down. A spring-tension mechanism inside the headrail holds the blind at whatever height you leave it. Vertical blinds use a wand to rotate and draw the vanes instead of a cord.

How do motorized blinds work, and do they need to be plugged in?

A small motor in the headrail moves the blind when you use a remote, wall switch, app or voice command. Not all motorized blinds need to be plugged in: we offer hardwired, rechargeable battery, replaceable battery and solar-powered options, so windows without a nearby outlet can still be motorized.

Are motorized blinds worth it?

For high windows, large windows, sliding doors and rooms with many blinds, most people find them well worth it: no reaching, no lifting, and you can open or close a whole room at once or on a schedule. For easy-to-reach standard windows, cordless is often all you need.

Which blinds are easiest to use?

Motorized blinds are the easiest: one button, no lifting or reaching. That makes them a good fit for anyone with limited mobility or arthritis, and for windows that are hard to get to.

How much do cordless and motorized blinds cost?

Cordless is the more affordable home option and motorized costs more, but the exact price depends on the window size, the blind style and fabric, and the motor's power option. Your free in-home consultation gives you an exact, no-obligation quote for each option.

Can I mix cordless and motorized blinds in the same home?

Yes, and many customers do: cordless in bedrooms and kitchens, motorized on the big living-room windows, stairwells or patio doors. We'll help you decide window by window.
